Neil Denby — Practical Experience and Documented Qualifications

Neil Denby, independent candidate for Hauraki-Waikato, brings decades of experience in aircraft electronics, technical training, maintenance management and electronic security.

He joined the Royal New Zealand Air Force in 1968, training as an Aircraft Radio Technician before the trade broadened into avionics. His career included service with 40 Squadron, instruction at Wigram from 1983 to 1986, and management of helicopter maintenance schedules at Hobsonville. He retired from the RNZAF in 1991.

Three certificates now support his technical background:

  • Radio Serviceman registration, issued by the Electricians’ Registration Board of New Zealand on 23 February 1979.
  • High Reliability Hand Soldering training, completed at the Royal Australian Air Force School of Radio. The certificate records a three-week course and is dated 9 May 1980.
  • National Certificate in Electronic Security (Systems Installation), Level 3, awarded in February 2013 through The Skills Organisation.

The earlier certificates record his name as Neil McLean Denby.

Neil’s account of his RNZAF work includes developing training for Air New Zealand personnel servicing USAF C130 aircraft supporting Antarctic research, introducing specialist soldering techniques and training fellow technicians. He also describes jointly developing a computerised system for managing electronic test and measurement equipment, subsequently used across RNZAF bases.

After leaving the Air Force, he joined Guardall Security in 1992 and worked on ASB Bank’s nationwide electronic security programme. He moved to Wormald Fire and Security in 1996 and later worked within ADT, servicing security and access-control systems for homes and large organisations. He retired in April 2016.

As a candidate, Neil proposes regular engagement with constituents, published progress reports and clear explanations of his parliamentary decisions. His policy interests include energy security, examining the restoration of refining at Marsden Point, a household dividend, farmers’ market tax relief and supermarket competition.

His stated principle is straightforward: an independent MP should remain answerable to the electorate, listen to its different communities and explain how—and why—he votes.
